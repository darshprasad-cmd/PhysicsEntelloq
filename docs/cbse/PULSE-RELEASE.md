## Bounded objective and references

Class 9 standard Science practical 10 (`s9-e10`) prescribes measuring pulse speed on a stretched string or slinky: [CBSE 2026–27, PDF page 19](https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/CurriculumMain27/SecPart1/ScienceSt_SecP1_2026-27.pdf#page=19). This interaction covers the **transverse-string alternative only**, not a longitudinal slinky experiment. It extends the existing preparation guide; the existing pulse animation, scientific engines and apparatus guides are untouched.

[NCERT Science Laboratory Manual, Class IX, Experiment 42, printed pages 168–172 / PDF pages 52–56](https://ncert.nic.in/pdf/publication/sciencelaboratorymanuals/classIX/science/ielm104.pdf#page=52) supports distance/time measurement with repeated travel. The manual counts one-way journeys. Here **N means round trips**, so there are **2N one-way journeys** and distance is **2NL**. Do not mix the conventions or average trials with different lengths or setup conditions. Physical work remains teacher-supervised; avoid excessive tension and keep the string clear of surfaces and knots.

## Original apparatus and scientific limits

The original schematic depicts a uniform, nondispersive teaching string between reflecting fixed ends. Two declared setups use speeds 3 and 4 m/s. These are chosen parameters, not material measurements or fitted tension/density data. Selected span is 1–5 m in half-metre steps; the learner may time 1, 3 or 5 round trips. No damping, pulse broadening, sag, elastic extension or real apparatus handling is reproduced.

The ideal replay reaches its endpoint at t = 2NL/v. The recorded clock is separate: three repeat trials apply deterministic teaching offsets of -0.04, +0.02 and +0.04 s, then display hundredths of a second. This is disclosed synthetic observation variation, not captured user reaction time or a calibrated stopwatch. The offsets have a nonzero mean; averaging need not recover the preset speed. Repeat observations with unchanged length, setup and journey count; calculate speed from distance divided by the mean displayed time. The spread is not a confidence interval.

For example, a synthetic L = 2 m and N = 3 corresponds to distance 12 m. If the clock reads 1.50 s, speed is 8.00 m/s. Using L/t would incorrectly give one sixth of that result. The example is independent of the two apparatus presets and is not a fabricated physical observation record.
